The week in brief

From Water Is Life to For The Ocean

As Climate Week NYC convened leaders from business, government, finance, conservation, culture and civil society, THE NAT hosted the NYC Solution Series: a flagship gala, a coral rave, two investor roundtables, two working dinners and a closing night for the ocean.

The week opened with THE NAT GALA, Water Is Life, and closed at the water's edge in Lower Manhattan. In between, investors, hyperscalers, farmers, scientists, founders and community voices sat at the same tables to work on water, food systems, digital infrastructure and regenerative investment.

Day01 Sunday 20 September
Flagship Gala Halo 28

THE NAT GALA 2026: Water Is Life

Nature returned to New York.

The second NAT GALA opened Climate Week NYC with a sensory journey through the full cycle of water, from salt marsh to glacier to reef. Across four acts, Sophie Hunter, Sabrina Elba, Tenzin Seldon, Dr Sylvia Earle, Dee Jay Two Bears, Rozzi and Pattie Gonia carried the room, timed to honour ten years since Standing Rock and the passing of the UN High Seas Treaty.

  • 5-minute standing ovation for Tenzin Seldon on Gross Domestic Regeneration
  • Voices: Gail Gallie, Sabrina Elba, Thomas Crowther, Emma Watson (on film), Tenzin Seldon, Jesper Brodin, Gabriela Hearst, Dr Sylvia Earle (on film), Lord Fakafānua and Titouan Bernicot
  • Performances: Sophie Hunter’s “Salt of the Earth”, Dee Jay Two Bears’ “What Came Before”, Rozzi and Pattie Gonia
  • Beneficiaries: Conservation International, Blue Marine Foundation and Open Planet

"Climate change isn't a moral failure, it's an accounting failure. Our economy does not account for nature because it does not see nature."

Tenzin Seldon, Founder & Managing Partner, Pulse Fund

Afterparty With Spotify & Coral Gardeners

NATCAP: Coral Rave

When the Gala ended, the reef came alive.

The late-night counterpart to THE NAT GALA turned a party into planting. Guests danced to a live set from DJ KP The Great, and every ticket planted a coral in THE NAT GARDEN, a new coral nursery grown by Coral Gardeners at Tiaia, Mo'orea, French Polynesia.

  • 374 corals planted, roughly 7% of the garden's 5,400-coral capacity
  • 302 guests, with nearly 1 in 3 ticket buyers planting two or more corals

Day02 Tuesday 22 September
Private Dinner Moss, New York

The Regenerative Table

Nature is infrastructure, and can be financed like it.

Investors, philanthropists, food-company sustainability leaders, conservationists and farmers gathered around two long tables, opening with a first look at Groundswell, Josh Tickell's new film. Three working models, from Kenya, Mozambique and the global soil economy, showed how restoration becomes stable, investable cash flow.

  • Speakers: Patricia Zurita (Conservation International), Edward Norton (Conservation Equity), Matt McLuckie (Karingani), Kristen Sparhawk (Bedari Collective) and Bill McGlashan (Oath), hosted by Jay Lipman and moderated by Hari Balasubramanian
  • Conservation Equity: luxury tourism profits flowing 100% to a conservation trust in Maasai conservancies
  • Karingani: a 150,000-hectare restored reserve that pays for itself
  • $711bn–$1.3tn annual nature funding gap, small relative to global capital markets

"Nature is producing a return. We just need to put the money in it."

Patricia Zurita, Conservation International

Day03 Wednesday 23 September
Roundtable I With CGC, ENVEST & Salesforce

Navigating Peak Water: Aligning Investors Around a New Normal

The capital is there. The connective tissue is not.

51 leaders from 48 organisations, from investors and lenders to corporate water leads, scientists and founders, scanned capital bottlenecks across the full water value chain and the solutions that could align capital behind water.

  • Key speakers: J. Carl Ganter (Circle of Blue), Claire Goelst (ENVEST), Phil Taylor (Mad Capital), Laura Zizzo (Estian Partners), Paul Fleming (WaterValue), Amanda Von Almen (Salesforce), Kirsten James (Ceres), Alix Lebec (LEBEC) and Eli Hopson (CGC), moderated by Jay Lipman
  • Death by pilot: water technologies are piloted again and again, but rarely bought
  • Every basin is different: plan and invest at basin scale, with shared intelligence
  • Value water, don't simply monetise it: full-value accounting changes the investment case

Roundtable II With the Patrick J. McGovern Foundation & C40 Cities

Harmonious Hyperscaling: Data Centres for a Nature-Positive Future

From differentiator to default.

53 hyperscalers, solutions providers, investors, funders, NGOs and community voices pressure-tested Pledge & Publish, a draft framework for nature-positive data centres, building on The True Nature of Hyperscaling at London Climate Week.

  • Key speakers: Nick Cain (the Patrick J. McGovern Foundation), Luke Upchurch (C40 Cities), Tripp Wall (Pantheon Generation), Gabriel Scheer (Elemental Impact), Kaitlin Chuzi (Microsoft) and Nicole Miller (Biomimicry 3.8), moderated by Jay Lipman
  • The build-out is the opportunity: landscape-scale restoration is a "rounding error" against project budgets
  • Community trust is now the binding constraint on what gets built
  • 50 cities now signed up to the C40 Global Urban Data Centre Pact

Working Dinner With ServiceNow · Ludlow House

Before the Storm: Building the Foundations for Sustainable AI

Getting it right, together.

Around 40–44 leaders from data centre development, energy, technology, cities, foundations and community organisations sat down for a working dinner, not a panel, with around $1 trillion expected to be spent on AI infrastructure in the next 12 months.

  • No one is in charge of getting it right: collaboration is the missing ingredient
  • Distributed energy as the community dividend: lower bills, not just benefit agreements
  • A convening hub for each bioregion, strongly backed by ServiceNow

"It felt like the best dialogue of any event this week."

Rex Martin, Sankari Studios

Day04 Thursday 24 September
Closing Night WSA, Water Street

For The Ocean: Closing Night of Climate Week NYC

An evening for the ocean.

Around 100 guests from the week's ocean community gathered at the water's edge for a reception, a candid panel on sport, culture and the changing ocean, and live music from Prazul, looking ahead to Oceans 2026 in Western Australia. Co-hosted with Progression, Oceans 2026, REEFLINE, ACTAI Global and ImpactAssets.

  • 3× WSL Longboard World Champion Rachael Tilly on stage with Bill Tai and Ximena Caminos
  • Culture moves people where panels can't: sport, art and music build a movement
  • Unlikely partnerships as the engine, and only the beginning

"Take care of nature as if your life depends on it. Because it does."

Dr Sylvia Earle
